Books & Personal Libraries

Australians borrowed almost 174 million items from public libraries last year, about six and a half for every person. But what fills our shelves, and how have our tastes changed? This page follows the books from Australia's libraries to the world's most-read titles, and asks whether the books we love most are the ones critics honour.

Australia's shelves

Public libraries are where many Australians find their books. How much we borrow, and how many libraries we have, depends a lot on where we live.

Queenslanders borrow the most, 7.8 items each a year, while Northern Territory residents borrow the least, 3.7. More branches don't always mean more borrowing: Western Australia has 233 library branches, more than South Australia and Tasmania combined, yet borrows only 5.5 items per person.

Collections are going digital

Australia's public libraries now hold almost 48 million items, up from 37 million five years ago. Most of that growth is digital: ebooks and audiobooks rose from 12% to 28% of collections, while physical collections barely changed.

What's on the shelf

The books Australians borrow and buy are largely the same global bestsellers read everywhere else. To see what fills that shared shelf, this section uses the 10,000 most-rated books on Goodreads, the world's largest book-rating website. Each book is given the genre readers most often shelved it under.

Fantasy leads the shelf

Fantasy is the most common genre among the world's most-read books, with 1,914 titles, followed closely by romance (1,728) and non-fiction (1,645). Poetry is the rarest, with just 143.

Some genres are rated more kindly

Each shape shows how a genre's books are spread across star ratings: the higher the peak, the more books scored there. Graphic novels are rated most generously. 82% of them average four stars or more, compared with just 35% of horror books.

Fantasy gets a quarter of all ratings

Wider columns are genres that receive more ratings, and the darker the top of a column, the more of those ratings are five stars. Fantasy makes up 19% of the books but attracts 26.5% of all 589 million ratings, and nearly half of them are five stars. Across every genre, only 5–9% of readers give a book one or two stars.

How tastes have changed

What we choose to read has shifted over the decades, and it is still shifting today.

Each column shows the genre mix of today's favourite books, grouped by the decade they were first published. Favourites from the 1950s to the 1970s are spread across non-fiction, children's books, science fiction and historical fiction. Since 2000, two genres dominate: in the 2010s, romance (28%) and fantasy (22%) make up half of all favourites. These are books still read today, so older decades show only the books that lasted.

Australians are searching for romance and fantasy

Google searches show the same shift happening in Australia right now. Searches for romance books roughly doubled between 2019 and 2025, and fantasy rose by about 70%, during the years BookTok (book videos on TikTok) drove sales of both genres. The scale runs from 0 to 100, where 100 is the busiest month. Google changed how it collects data in 2022, but the rise continues well after that change.

Popular vs loved

You might expect the most popular books to also be the best-loved. They often are not.

Six in ten books sit between 3.8 and 4.2 stars

Twilight has 3.9 million ratings but only 3.57 stars, while The Complete Calvin and Hobbes has far fewer readers and 4.82. Sydney-born Markus Zusak's The Book Thief manages both: 1.2 million ratings and 4.36 stars.".

Even favourite authors have misses

Readers rate the book, not the name. J.K. Rowling's books range from 3.28 stars for The Casual Vacancy, her first novel for adults, to 4.61 for Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows. Stephen King spans almost as wide a range, while Dan Brown is the most consistent, between 3.60 and 3.85.
